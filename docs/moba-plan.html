Keeping it fair

No hero gets to be the strong one. Every number lives in one hero catalog, and each hero spends the same power budget: base stats sit in one shared band, and extra damage is paid for with a weakness.

Hero | Strong at | Weak at Haru | Burst on one target, escaping through clones | Long fights, since energy runs dry. Falls off late Ryuka | Safe damage from range, late game | Lowest health. Rooted while the beam channels. Slow mana early Torvik | One-on-one duels, sticking to targets | No range. Stamina runs out in long chases Seo Dan | Objectives and fights near camps | Weak ganks until his soldiers stack up Brakk | Starting fights, protecting allies | Low damage, long cooldowns From milestone 2, a test mode runs hundreds of bot-vs-bot matches and reports each hero's win rate. A hero outside 45 to 55% gets tuned in the catalog, not in code. New names, new silhouettes, new colors, and no signature lines, outfits, or hairstyles copied from the shows. An archetype such as a clone ninja or a titan shifter is fair to use. An exact character is not, and can get the game taken down when it gets noticed.

Map layout

Three lanes run between the bases at opposite corners, and the river crosses on the other diagonal. Three things set it apart from League's map: mid lane runs straight through a **crash ring**, a shallow round dip where the river meets mid; each team has two **launch pads** in its jungle that fling you to the river; and each lane has two towers, not three. The mothership wreck sits in the top river pit, where the late boss lives. The map mirrors across the line between the two bases, so both teams get the same layout.

Getting stronger

  • **Levels 1 to 15.** Experience comes from nearby alien and monster deaths and from takedowns. The curve is tuned so heroes reach 15 around the 18-minute mark. Points go into Q W E (4 ranks each), and R ranks up at 6, 10, and 14.
  • **Gold.** Last hits on aliens and monsters, kills and assists, towers, and a small income every second.
  • **Items.** Bought only at the fountain, 6 slots. Boots, potions, 4 standalone items, and the 8 pair items below. No recipe trees.
  • **Respawns** grow with level, from 6 seconds to about 35 seconds.

Item pairs

Each pair item is good on its own. Owning both items of a pair unlocks its **pair bonus**, which pushes you toward a different way of winning. Only one pair bonus can be active, so choosing a pair is a commitment.

The four standalone items: **Beetle Carapace** (armor that reflects some basic attack damage), **Void Shard** (ability power that cuts healing on hit), **Swift Boots** (the upgrade to basic boots), and **Starfall Pendant** (magic resist and a one-time shield when dropping low).

Runes

Before a match you pick one rune from each of three rows. Every rune is small, a few percent at most, and most are sidegrades: they change what you are good at, not how strong you are. That keeps Robux purchases from deciding matches.

Row | Runes (two are unlocked from the start) Offense | Sharpened (+3% attack) · Focused (+3% ability power) · Hunter's Edge (+5% damage to monsters) · Executioner (+5% damage to heroes under 35% health) · Siege Breaker (+6% damage to structures) Defense | Plated (+4 armor) · Warded (+4 magic resist) · Second Wind (heal 2% health after taking hero damage, 10 s cooldown) · Steady (+8% resistance to slows) · Bulwark (+5% shield strength) Utility | Swift (+2% move speed) · Haste (+3 ability haste) · Wellspring (+10% resource regen) · Bounty (+1 gold per last hit) · Spell Tuner (-5% summoner spell cooldowns)
  • **Earned by playing.** Each match gives account experience, win or lose, and every account level unlocks the next rune. All 15 are unlocked after about 25 matches.
  • **Or unlocked now.** A one-time Robux developer product unlocks every rune at once. It gives no rune anyone else can't earn.
  • Runes need saved player data, so they arrive with the lobby in M3.

How it runs

A match can hold about 100 live units at once: 10 heroes, up to 70 aliens, 26 monsters, and 20 structures. Roblox characters (Humanoids) are too heavy for that, especially on phones, so the plan runs the match as a server-owned simulation.

Server-owned unit simulation [recommended] | the server moves every unit as plain data on a fixed tick and streams compact snapshots, while clients only draw the models + About 100 units stay cheap, because there is no Humanoid physics + Fog of war is secure: the server leaves hidden enemies out of each team's stream, so exploits can't reveal them + Players can't speed-hack or teleport their hero - We write movement, pathing around walls, and unit spacing ourselves - Ping delays your own hero, so the client moves it ahead and the server corrects it Roblox characters and Humanoid NPCs | each hero is a normal player character and minions are Humanoid NPCs + Quick to start, with animation and physics built in - About 100 Humanoids is heavy on the server and on phones - Roblox sends every enemy's position to every client, so fog can be bypassed - Each client owns its character's physics, so speed hacks are possible Player [user] -> HeroControlSystem: click, tap, or button HeroControlSystem -> Command remote [queue]: move, attack, cast Command remote -> UnitSystem [svc]: checked order BotSystem -> UnitSystem: bot orders MinionWaveSystem -> UnitSystem: spawns aliens JungleSystem -> UnitSystem: spawns monsters StructureSystem -> UnitSystem: towers and cores UnitSystem -> GoldSystem: last hits, kills UnitSystem -> MatchSystem: core destroyed UnitSystem -> Snapshot stream [cache]: 20 per second Snapshot stream -> UnitVisualsSystem: draws and smooths

Systems never require each other directly. Each one's Implementation holds the mediators that connect it to the others, following the Module Loader layout in this repo. The heroes' kits live as data and ability handlers in `HeroSystem`'s Implementation, so `UnitSystem` and `AbilitySystem` stay generic.

Player [user] -> HeroControlSystem [client]: right-clicks the ground HeroControlSystem -> HeroControlSystem: shows the click marker, starts walking at once HeroControlSystem -> Command [remote]: FireServer(move, point) Command -> HeroControlServerService [server]: OnServerEvent note HeroControlServerService: rate-limits, checks the hero is alive and the point is on the map HeroControlServerService -> UnitSystem [server]: SetOrder(hero, move) UnitSystem -> UnitSystem: finds a path, steps each tick UnitSystem --> UnitVisualsSystem [client]: snapshots for this team UnitVisualsSystem -> UnitVisualsSystem: corrects your hero, smooths the rest

Risks

The heroes stay safe only while their names, faces, outfits, and signature moves stay original. Review each hero's final art against its source show before release. Selling runes means money can touch gameplay, which the first plan ruled out. It stays fair only while every rune is small, mostly a sidegrade, and earnable in about 25 matches. Ranked (M4) should keep that rule. About 100 units with health bars and effects is heavy for low-end phones. Visuals use pooling, and the roblox-performance rules apply from MB-2 onward, with a profile on a real phone at the end of milestone 1. With few players, most slots go to bots, so bot quality decides whether the game is fun at launch. MB-14 gets real time, not a stub. The server owns movement, so your own hero would lag by your ping. The client starts moving it at once and eases toward the server's position, the way most MOBAs do. A MOBA HUD is crowded. The game-ui-design rules for thumb reach and text size guide MB-16 and MB-23.
